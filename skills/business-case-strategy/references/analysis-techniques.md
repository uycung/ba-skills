# Supporting techniques: SWOT, root cause, KPIs, post-deployment measurement

## SWOT (BABOK 10.46)
Assesses the strengths, weaknesses (internal), opportunities and threats (external) of an organization, product, process, project or person. Language that is short, specific, realistic and evidence-based. **Needs a clear context**, otherwise the result rambles and contains irrelevant factors (10.46.4). Start with opportunities and threats to set the context before considering strengths and weaknesses.
- S: things the team does well (people, processes, systems, customer relationships...). W: things done poorly. O: external factors that can be exploited. T: external factors that can do harm.
- Strategy matrix: SO (use strengths to seize opportunities), ST (use strengths to counter threats, possibly turning threats into opportunities), WO (use opportunities to overcome weaknesses, is a new capability worth developing), WT (restructure to avoid threats, should we exit the market: the worst case).
- Skill: label every item [Stated]/[Inferred]/[Assumption] with a source; point out which items need data to be checked. A SWOT result is a high-level view and does not replace detailed analysis.

## Root cause analysis (10.40)
A systematic examination of the origin of a problem instead of only its consequences; it can be reactive (the cause of a problem that is happening) or preventive (areas where a problem could arise). Categories of causes: human, physical, organizational.
Four activities: define the problem, collect data (nature, extent, where, when), identify causes, identify corrective actions.
- **Fishbone** (Ishikawa): the problem at the fish's head, branches by cause category (for example people, process, tools, policy), sub-branches for deeper causes. The result gives only **potential** causes; check them against data before discussing solutions.
- **5 Why**: ask "why" repeatedly until reaching the root cause; the number of times can be fewer or more than 5; used alone or with a fishbone; suits cases with human interaction factors.
- Limits: needs trained people so they do not stop at symptoms; complex problems easily lead to dead ends.
- Skill: do not conclude a root cause from a single conversation; list the cause hypotheses + the data needed to check them.

## Metrics and KPIs (10.28)
- An indicator is a specific measure showing progress towards an objective; a metric is the quantified level of an indicator; a KPI is an indicator that measures progress towards a strategic objective.
- **A good indicator has six characteristics**: clear, relevant, economical (reasonable cost of collection), adequate, quantifiable (independently verifiable), credible (based on evidence).
- When direct measurement is impossible: use a proxy indicator (for example the contract renewal rate in place of a satisfaction survey).
- When setting up an indicator: source, collection method, collector, cost, frequency, difficulty.
- A metric needs a **baseline** and a **target** over a period of time; it can be a point, a threshold or a range.
- Indicator quality: reliability, validity, timeliness.
- Skill rule: for an objective without a baseline, write the question "what is the current baseline and where is it measured?", do not invent one.

## Measuring value after deployment (chapter 8)
- 8.1: define measures of solution performance (quantitative and qualitative), confirm them with stakeholders (the sponsor usually decides which measures are chosen), collect them (sample size, frequency, currency).
- 8.2: analyzing the measures requires understanding the potential value stakeholders expect; measures rarely lead by themselves to a decision about value.
- 8.5: recommend actions when actual value differs from potential: adjust the measures; do nothing when the change in value is small relative to effort or the risk is higher; organizational change; reduce interface complexity; remove redundancy and waste; identify additional capabilities; retire (replace/remove) the solution, weighing rising operating costs against the original investment.
- Skill: a business case should come with a **measurement plan**: indicator, baseline, target, when measured, who is responsible.
