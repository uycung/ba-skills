# Need, current state, future state

## Business need (BABOK 6.1.4.1)
- A need is a strategic problem or opportunity of the organization; it often starts from customer complaints, lost revenue, a market opportunity. It can arise top-down (strategic objectives), bottom-up (process problems), from middle management (need for information/functions), or from outside (customer needs, competition).
- **How the need is defined determines** which options are considered, who is asked, which approaches are assessed; a need is always stated from the organization's viewpoint, not that of a single stakeholder.
- When the need comes with a predetermined solution: ask again about the assumptions and constraints.
- Consider: the current negative impact (quantifiable: lost revenue, inefficiency, dissatisfied customers, morale), expected benefits, how fast to address it and the **cost of doing nothing**, the origin of the problem.
- Questions: who says this is a problem? what is the evidence? how is it measured? if nothing is done, what happens in 12 months (numbers, source)?

## Current state (6.1)
Only enough detail to confirm the need and/or the strategy; described at several levels (whole organization down to small components); the current state does not stand still while a change is being prepared. The elements:
| Element | Consider |
|---|---|
| Business need | as above |
| Organizational structure and culture | Reporting relationships, communication channels; culture (beliefs, values, norms): does the culture need to change, do stakeholders understand why the current state is as it is, do they find it acceptable |
| Capabilities and processes | Capabilities (what the organization does, knowledge, products, how decisions are made) or processes (end-to-end); the capability view suits finding innovative solutions that combine existing capabilities; the process view suits improving performance |
| Technology and infrastructure | Information systems, physical infrastructure |
| Policies | Limits on the scope of decision-making, usually constraints on the solution |
| Business architecture | The elements support one another; do not assess any part in complete isolation |
| Internal assets | Financial, patents, reputation... |
| External influences | Industry structure, competitors, customers, suppliers, political and regulatory environment, technology, macro-economy |
Supporting analysis frameworks (BABOK 6.1.5 names them): 5 Forces, PEST, STEEP, CATWOE. Known by name only; there is no detailed guidance in the documents that were read.
Wording may differ by type of organization: a country has citizens, not customers.
Current-state data needs: a source (interview, document, operational data) and a label.

## Future state (6.2)
The goal is **just enough detail** to: compare competing strategies, define outcomes that satisfy the need, define the scope of the solution space, assess value, reach consensus among key stakeholders. Describe what is new, removed, changed.
| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Goals and objectives | Goal: long-term, qualitative, a state to reach or maintain. Objective: specific, with an indicator. Example goal: increase the number of high-value customers → objective: increase the number of high-value customers in age group X by Y% within Z months (restating BABOK's example; you fill in your own figures) |
| SMART check | Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bounded |
| Scope of the solution space | The kinds of solution considered (organization, capability, technology, policy, product, relations with outside parties); not necessarily proportional to the size of the change. When several futures meet the objectives, decide on quantitative/qualitative value, time to achieve, opportunity cost |
| Constraints | Budget, resources, skills, stakeholder groups that must not be affected, regulatory compliance, technical constraints; must be verified precisely |
| Assumptions | The strategy rests on assumptions, especially in an uncertain environment; they must be stated with a way to test them early so you can change direction/stop |
| Potential value | The net benefit of the solution after operating costs; must exceed doing nothing, except in mandatory cases (new regulation, competition) where value may be lower but it still has to be done |
| Resources | Existing and additional needed |
Reaching the objectives is not enough to justify moving to the future state; the potential value must be enough to justify the cost.
When value is hard to predict: define the future state through performance measures and use the change strategy to try several options (6.2.2).

## Quick questions for the user (at most 4)
1. What exactly is the problem/opportunity and what evidence shows it is real?
2. What does success look like, measured by which indicator, within how long?
3. Are there hard constraints (budget, deadline, regulation, technology)?
4. Who decides, and who is affected most?
