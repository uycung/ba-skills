# Gap, risk, change strategy

## Gap analysis (BABOK 6.4.4.2)
- Compare current and future capabilities; **both states must be defined**; use the same description technique for both so they can be compared.
- If the organization can meet the need with existing capabilities, the change is small or unnecessary; otherwise a strategy is needed to create or improve capabilities.
- Capabilities considered: processes, functions, lines of business, organizational structure, locations, data and information, staff capability, knowledge and skills, training, facilities, applications, technology infrastructure.
- Table: Capability | Current | Future | Gap | How to close it (stage) | Source.
- Do not fill in the "Future" or "Gap" column if no definition exists yet.

## Organizational readiness (6.4.4.3)
Assess the ability to carry out, **use and sustain** the change and to receive the value: cultural readiness, operational readiness, time from deployment to value, supporting resources. Ask the user instead of inferring; label the answers.

## Risk (6.3, 10.38)
A risk = an uncertain event or condition that can negatively affect value. (BABOK chapter 6.3: opportunities are recorded as needs; risk is used for uncertain events that cause negative outcomes.)
**Analyze each risk by**: the consequence if it occurs, the impact, the likelihood, the time frame in which it could occur (6.3.2).
**Risk register** (example structure from BABOK 10.38): #, condition ("if…"), consequence ("then…"), likelihood, impact, risk level, proposed response, owner, residual likelihood and impact.
- Likelihood: a numeric probability or low/medium/high. Impact is described in terms of cost, time, scope, quality, reputation, compliance, social; thresholds are chosen by the organization, usually 3-5 levels. **Do not invent thresholds**; ask whether the organization has a standard scale.
- Constraints, assumptions and dependencies can be rewritten as risks (6.3.4.2).
- Responses: reduce likelihood, reduce consequence, eliminate the source, avoid (do not do it), share with another party, accept (sometimes increasing risk to seize an opportunity).
- **Risk appetite** (6.3.4.4): risk-averse (avoid or invest more, accept lower value), neutral (accept when it does not lead to a loss even if the risk occurs), risk-seeking (accept more risk to obtain higher value); varies over time; the highest risks usually must be handled regardless of appetite.
- **Recommendations** (6.3.4.5), usually one of: pursue the benefit regardless of the risk; pursue and invest to reduce the risk; seek to increase the benefit to outweigh the risk; manage and optimize the opportunity; do not pursue.
- An accepted risk must have someone watching it and (where needed) a plan before it occurs.
- Risk information: **estimated figures are judgments** (label [Assumption]); add lessons from similar projects if available.

## Options and change strategy (6.4)
A change strategy describes: the context, the options considered, the justification for the chosen option, investment and resources, how value will be realized, key stakeholders, transition states.
**Finding options** by brainstorming and consulting SMEs; sources of ideas: previous changes, strategies in other markets, competitors.
**Selection criteria** (6.4.4.4): organizational readiness, large costs and investments, time, fit with business objectives, when value arrives, opportunity cost. Build a business case for each option when it supports the decision (10.7).
**Transition and release stages** (6.4.4.5): the future state is usually reached in several steps; for each stage say which parts of the solution are done/not done and what value is received/not received; release decisions weigh budget, deadlines, resources, training schedules, the capacity to absorb change, organizational policy. Link to `requirements-prioritize` for the order of requirements.
**Solution scope** (6.4.4.1): described enough for stakeholders to understand the new capability and how it achieves the future objectives; can be described in terms of capabilities, functions, technology, locations, rules, processes, organization, events...; includes what is out of scope for clarity.
