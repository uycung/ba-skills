# Engagement, communication and stakeholder risk plans

## Engagement plan (BABOK 3.2.4.2)
Collaboration can be spontaneous but is mostly planned: activities and outcomes are defined beforehand. It may differ between internal and external stakeholders and by BA activity.
Consider: **time and frequency**, **place**, **tools** (wiki, online communities), **means** (in person or remote), **stakeholder preferences**. Revisit when the factors change.
Suggested table: Stakeholder/group | Purpose of collaboration | Activity (interview, workshop, review, survey) | Frequency/timing | Means | Preparation needed | Risks | Owner.
Choose the technique per `requirements-elicitation` (references/techniques.md, workshops-surveys.md): few people/sensitive → interview; many people needing consensus → workshop; large and distributed → survey.

## Communication plan (3.2.4.3)
For each flow, assess: **the content to be communicated**, **the means** (written or spoken), **the audience**, **the timing**, **the frequency**, **the geographic location** of recipients, **the level of detail**, **the level of formality**. Build and review it with stakeholders so expectations are met.
Suggested table: Information | Audience | Purpose | Means | Frequency | Level of detail | Sender | Channel/where stored.
Link with `requirements-document` (requirements packages by audience: BABOK 4.4 asks who the reader is, what they need, preferred style, legal/contractual constraints).

## Stakeholder risks (3.2.6, 6.3)
State as a risk when: a key stakeholder is heavily involved but has a negative attitude (needs a collaboration approach that raises cooperation); the influence needed ≫ the influence held; the decision-maker has no time; the representative changes midway; it is unclear who decides.
For each risk: condition + consequence + likelihood + impact + proposed response + owner who watches it. The full structure of risk assessment is in `business-case-strategy`. Here we stop at a preliminary list.

## Quick plan check
- Every stakeholder in the "high influence" corner has at least one regular collaboration activity.
- Every deliverable needing approval has a specific decision-maker (A) and a deadline.
- No stakeholder only receives information when they should be consulted (C vs I).
- The plan matches the actual meeting cadence; do not promise a schedule nobody can keep.
