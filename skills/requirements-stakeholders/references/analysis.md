# Stakeholder analysis: the list and characteristics

## 1. Building the list (BABOK 3.2.4.1, 10.43.3.1)
The goal is a complete list; missing a group means missing a source of requirements, and needs found late force redoing work that is done or in progress and raise cost.
**Where to look** (BABOK):
- Organization charts and business processes: the first source for internal stakeholders.
- The sponsor may name additional people.
- External: existing contracts, prospective suppliers, regulators and governing bodies; shareholders, customers, suppliers.
- Process models (classify by the system that supports the process), organization models (units/people with their own needs), scope models (stakeholders outside scope but interacting).
**Techniques for creating the list**: brainstorming, interviews, mind map.
**Classification to catch omissions**: the BABOK standard role list: business analyst, customer, domain subject matter expert, end user, implementation subject matter expert, operational support, project manager, regulator, sponsor, supplier, tester. For each role ask "does this project have one, who is it?". A role that does not apply is recorded as "not applicable" with a reason, not silently dropped.
Additional suggestions from this skill (beyond BABOK): groups indirectly affected (support team, finance, legal, security), owners of related systems/data.

## 2. Characteristics to analyze (3.2.4.1, 10.43.2)
| Characteristic | Ask |
|---|---|
| Role | Where do they contribute to the initiative? One person can hold several roles; the same job title can have different roles. |
| Attitude | About the goals and the solution, about BAs in general, level of interest, about the sponsor, about other members, about team collaboration. |
| Decision authority | Who decides what about BA activities, deliverables, changes; who approves. |
| Power/influence | The influence needed for the change to succeed versus the influence the stakeholder has; informal channels of influence. |
| Impact | How the change affects them (work, process, data, skills). |
| Availability | Their time, location, time zone, priorities. |
Record each cell with the label [Stated]/[Inferred]/[Assumption] and the source.

## 3. When to revisit
A change of scope, a change of organization, a new sponsor, a change of phase, an escalated issue. The model/list is alive; it is not done once.

## 4. Large scale
Complexity grows non-linearly as the number of stakeholders goes from a handful to dozens, hundreds or thousands (3.2.2); then group stakeholders by layer and by representative, use surveys/personas instead of interviewing each person, and record who represents whom.

## 5. Signs the list is incomplete
- A business rule or process flow has no owner.
- A regulation/law is named in the documents but no one is responsible for compliance.
- External parties interacting with the system (partners, agencies, customers) do not appear.
- End users are represented only by managers, with no one who does the work every day.
