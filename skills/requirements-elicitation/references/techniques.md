# Choosing elicitation techniques

## Three styles of elicitation (BABOK 4.2.2, p. 61)
- **Collaborative**: direct interaction with stakeholders, drawing on their experience and judgment.
- **Research**: studying existing documents/sources, possibly including analysis of historical data.
- **Experiments**: finding information nobody has yet through controlled trials (observation, proof of concept, prototype).

## Considerations when choosing (BABOK 4.1.4.2, p. 58)
Cost and time, the type of information source and how accessible it is, organizational culture, the desired outcome, stakeholders' needs and schedules, location (co-located or distributed). Several techniques are often used for one activity; adjust as the situation changes.

## Suggested pairing of situations with techniques (summarized from the technique list in BABOK 4.2.6; the "situation" column is the skill's suggestion)
| Situation | Suggested technique |
|---|---|
| Understanding the current process | Observation, interviews, document analysis, process modelling |
| Individual, sensitive opinions needed | One-to-one interview |
| Consensus among several parties, fixing scope | Facilitated workshop |
| Many people, raw numbers needed | Survey/questionnaire |
| Interface/experience unclear | Prototype |
| Legacy system/contract/regulation exists | Document analysis, interface analysis |
| Need to find hidden rules | Business rules analysis |
Logistics to prepare (BABOK 4.1.4.3): objective, participants and roles, resources (people, rooms, tools), location, communication channels, technique, language used. Supporting documents: system documents, rules, policies, regulations, contracts (4.1.4.4).

## Interview process (BABOK 10.25.3, pp. 291–293; read only from the questions section onwards)
Opening: state the purpose, confirm roles and concerns, explain how information will be recorded and shared. During the session: stay on the objective and prepared questions but adapt, listen actively and confirm back, take notes or record (needs a note-taker), deal with concerns immediately or note them for follow-up. Closing: ask what was missed, give contact details, summarize, state how the results will be used, say thanks. After the session: organize and confirm with the interviewee as soon as possible. Limits named in BABOK: time-consuming, needs training, documents may carry the interviewer's interpretation, risk of leading.

## Confirming results (BABOK 4.3.4)
Compare with the original source and with other elicitation results; contradictions often surface when specifications and models are built. Confirmation is much lighter than the formal review in the analysis phase. If results are inconsistent, more elicitation may be needed.
