# Guidance on using the quality model per ISO/IEC 25002:2024

A paraphrased summary of 25002:2024 (first edition, current), checked on 2026-10-26 against the copy supplied by the user. Cite section numbers when quoting; do not copy verbatim. Items 1 to 3 and 5 to 8 come from the standard; the "Skill convention" lines are not from the standard.

1. **Tailoring (§7.1)**: the standard says quality models may be modified or customized at the **sub-characteristic** level, not at the characteristic level, for a specific context of use. It does not use the words "add, remove or rename" and does not require a recorded reason.
   *Skill convention:* when you tailor, record what changed and why; every "not applicable" sub-characteristic needs a short reason.
2. **Trade-offs (§7.1)**: characteristics can pull against each other (the standard's examples: security lowering usability; designing for performance raising complexity and lowering maintainability). State the conflicting pair and let the PO decide.
3. **Prioritization by context (§7.4)**: not every characteristic matters equally; rank by product type, users, risk. The standard's examples: reliability and security for online transactions; maintainability, flexibility and compatibility for enterprise IT services; performance efficiency and interaction capability for user interfaces. Use `product-profiles.md` as a starting point.
4. **Conditional quality requirements (§7.4)**: a requirement can differ by context of use. The standard's examples: response time with different criteria for consumers on different bandwidths or with accessibility needs; recovery times in an SLA that differ by subscription level. When one NFR has several contexts, write one row per context instead of one averaged threshold.
5. **Extending the model (§7.5)**: models should be extended when technology and usage create new quality goals. The standard's example for cognitive systems adds self-learning capability and self-learning speed as sub-characteristics. Sub-sub-characteristics may be defined for one context of use but are not part of the standard model. Useful for AI products: if the 9 characteristics do not capture a quality that matters (for example learning behaviour), say so and propose a documented extension at sub-characteristic level.
6. **The BA's role (§8.1, §8.2.2)**: business analysts elicit system requirements and should anticipate non-functional quality requirements from stakeholder needs and context of use, from the requirements phase instead of finding them in testing. Quality requirements can be stated qualitatively with a quality model and quantitatively with measures; the more quantitative, the greater the assurance (§8.2.2).
7. **Measurement (§8.2.5)**: ISO/IEC 25023 enumerates measures for product quality characteristics and sub-characteristics; 25024 does so for data quality (ISO/IEC 25012). A draft DIS 25023:2026 was noted earlier and is not in the supplied sources. When writing NFRs, each requirement needs a measure, a threshold and a measurement method, even if the exact codes of 25023 are not used.
8. **Related standards (Introduction, §7.2)**: the product quality model (25010) is one of several models. 25011 (IT service quality), 25012 (data quality), 25019 (quality in use). Measurement division 25020 to 25029; quality requirements division 25030 to 25039 (25030 describes how quality models support quality requirements); evaluation division 25040 to 25049. For data-heavy or AI products, ask whether data quality (25012) needs its own requirements, and for services, whether 25011 applies; this skill writes product quality NFRs only and sends the rest to the BA to specify separately.
